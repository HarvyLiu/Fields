I signed up to help with reading and spent most of the six weeks learning that
the assessment was measuring something other than reading.

The students who scored lowest were not the ones who understood the story. They
were the ones translating inside their heads before answering, and the test did
not pay for the translation. One boy, Marco, retold an entire chapter in Spanish
with better comprehension than I had — and scored two levels below grade.

> "He's at a level 8." The level is real. It is also measuring how fast a
> sentence crosses a border.

These are field notes, not a study. But I quoted the teacher's assessments next
to the children's retellings, and the gap between the two columns is the whole
argument.
