My mother says "since I am here" where a native speaker would say "since I've
been here." For eleven years I thought that was a mistake. It is not a mistake.
It is a tense that English does not have: the continuous state of having arrived
and continuing to arrive.

> We do not drop articles because we cannot hear them. We drop them because we
> are carrying a language that does not have them, and we are doing double duty
> in one mouth.

The essay argues that assimilation is measured in grammar before it is measured
in anything else — that the first thing a child learns to correct is not their
accent but their parent's syntax. I interviewed six families, including my own,
and quoted them without cleaning up the quotes. That editorial choice took three
revisions and a long argument with my teacher, who wanted brackets.

## Reflection

I had written about my family before, but always from above, as an observer with
a better sentence. This is the first piece I wrote from inside it.
